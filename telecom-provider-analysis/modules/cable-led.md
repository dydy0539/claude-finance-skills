# Cable-Led Provider Module

Applies to **Comcast and Charter** — providers whose primary connectivity business is residential broadband over HFC/fiber, with mobile (MVNO on Verizon's network) attached as a bundle anchor, plus business services and (declining) video/voice.

The economics differ meaningfully from the Big 3. Use this module for cable connectivity providers; ignore wireless-led metrics that don't apply.

---

## C1. Residential Broadband

The core engine of cable economics. Always lead with this.

- **Residential broadband net adds** — absolute number; YoY trend; this is the most market-sensitive number for cable stocks
- **Total broadband customer base** — cumulative; note recent peak vs. current trend
- **Broadband ARPU** — YoY change in dollars and %; cable broadband ARPU has historically been a steady grower (~3–4%)
- **Speed mix** — % of base on gigabit speeds; mix shift drives ARPU
- **Promotional intensity** — note management commentary on competitive promotions, especially in fiber-overbuilt markets and FWA-pressured markets

The competitive context for broadband net adds is now structurally different: **cable has been losing net adds to fiber overbuilders (AT&T Fiber, Frontier, Brightspeed, Ziply, etc.) and to FWA (Verizon, T-Mobile)**. A return to positive broadband adds is thesis-positive; continued losses are the bear case.

## C2. Mobile (MVNO)

Cable mobile runs on Verizon's network as MVNOs (Xfinity Mobile for Comcast, Spectrum Mobile for Charter), with WiFi offload and increasing CBRS deployment.

- **Mobile lines** — total cumulative + net adds for the period
- **Mobile line economics** — note that cable reports lines, not "postpaid phone" the same way Big 3 do
- **Mobile ARPU** — typically lower than Big 3 postpaid ARPU because mobile is priced as a broadband-retention tool, not a primary profit center
- **Mobile attach rate** = mobile lines ÷ broadband customers — this is the bundle penetration metric
- **CBRS / offload progress** — cable companies are deploying licensed CBRS spectrum to offload MVNO traffic and improve unit economics; report progress
- **Mobile contribution to broadband retention** — management often discloses churn benefit of bundled customers

Cable mobile is typically a **share gainer in postpaid** because the bundle is structurally cheaper than Big 3 standalone wireless. Track cable mobile net adds against the Big 3's postpaid net adds — they're often inversely correlated.

## C3. Video & Voice (Legacy)

These are in secular decline but still cash-generative; track for trajectory.

- **Video subscribers** — net losses for the period; YoY rate of decline
- **Voice subscribers** — same; voice declines faster than video typically
- **Video ARPU** — often flat-to-rising despite sub losses (rate increases), but contribution declines as base shrinks
- Management commentary on cord-cutting pace and any product/bundle reactions
- **Video margin** — video gross margin is structurally low/declining due to programming costs; cable companies have been deemphasizing video

For the Snapshot Card, video and voice are reported as legacy lines but don't get top-billing — focus is broadband + mobile + business.

## C4. Business Services (SMB + Enterprise)

A growing, high-margin segment that's structurally important to the cable thesis.

- **Business services revenue** and **YoY growth %**
- **SMB vs. Enterprise** split if disclosed — SMB is typically the larger contributor and grows faster
- **Mid-market and Enterprise** — Comcast Business and Spectrum Business have been moving up-market with managed services, SD-WAN, security
- Comparison to peers: cable business services compete with AT&T Business Wireline and Verizon Business in many segments

## C5. Convergence (Mobile-Broadband Bundle)

For cable, convergence is **broadband-anchored** — mobile is added to a broadband customer to reduce churn and increase household ARPU. This is the inverse of Big 3 logic (where mobile is the anchor and broadband is the add-on).

- **Mobile attach rate** to broadband (from C2) — central convergence metric
- **Bundled customer churn** vs. broadband-only churn — convergence's churn benefit
- **ARPU lift** for converged households if disclosed
- Strategic narrative: is mobile aggressively priced to drive broadband retention, or is mobile being repositioned as a profit center as scale grows?

## C6. Network Capex & Upgrade Cycle

Cable's capex profile is different from wireless — driven by network upgrades (DOCSIS 4.0, fiber overbuild/extension, rural buildouts) rather than spectrum and 5G.

- **Total capex** and **capex intensity** (capex ÷ revenue)
- **Cable connectivity capex** specifically (separate from content/parks for Comcast)
- **Network upgrade status**: DOCSIS 3.1 / mid-split / DOCSIS 4.0 progress; HFC-to-FTTP migration in select markets
- **Rural buildouts** — Charter has been aggressive on rural with subsidies (RDOF, BEAD)
- Capex trajectory: management guidance for capex peak/decline post-upgrade

## C7. Free Cash Flow

Cable historically generates very high FCF margins — this is the cornerstone of the cable thesis.

- **FCF after capex** — absolute and FCF margin (often 15–25% of revenue, structurally higher than Big 3)
- For Comcast, separate **Connectivity & Platforms FCF** from total (NBCU and parks have different cash dynamics)
- **FCF growth YoY** — the key metric supporting buybacks
- **Working capital and one-time items** — flag distortions

## C8. Balance Sheet & Capital Return

Cable companies have historically been **buyback-heavy** rather than dividend-heavy (Charter especially), making capital return a major thesis driver.

- **Net debt** and **net debt / EBITDA leverage** — Charter has run notably elevated leverage (~4x); Comcast is more conservative (~2.5x)
- Compare to **stated leverage target** if any
- **Buyback pace** — total $ repurchased in the period; YoY share count reduction
- **Dividend** (Comcast pays one; Charter doesn't) — DPS, yield, payout ratio
- **Capital allocation hierarchy** stated by management

## C9. Non-Connectivity (Comcast-Specific)

For Comcast only, briefly note NBCU, Peacock, and theme parks as context — but don't anchor the snapshot on them. They're material to consolidated financials but not to the connectivity competitive analysis.

- **NBCU revenue / EBITDA trend** — one-line summary; flag any material divergence from connectivity trend
- **Peacock subs / losses** — one-line; structurally a drag on consolidated EBITDA but narrowing
- **Parks performance** — one-line; macro-sensitive

For Charter, this section is n/a — Charter is pure-play connectivity.
