# Wireless-Led Provider Module

Applies to **AT&T, Verizon, and T-Mobile** — providers whose primary business is wireless service, with broadband/fiber/FWA as a secondary (but strategically central) layer.

Use this module to determine which sections to include in the Provider Snapshot Detail (Part B) and which metrics to populate in the Provider Snapshot Card (Part A).

---

## W1. Wireless Subscribers

The market-sensitive heart of every Big 3 quarter. Always lead with this.

- **Postpaid phone net adds** — absolute number for the period; YoY trend (better/worse than year-ago); compare to peers when known
- **Postpaid phone churn** — report as %; <1% is best-in-class; rising churn is a red flag for retention or competitive intensity
- **Postpaid other net adds** — tablets, wearables, IoT, connected cars; report separately so phone net adds isn't muddled
- **Prepaid net adds and churn** — directionally important (T-Mobile via Metro is largest prepaid player); less monetizable than postpaid
- **Total wireless net adds** — sum across all categories
- **Sequential vs YoY trend** — note any inflection (e.g., "second straight quarter of decelerating net adds")

## W2. ARPU & Service Revenue

- **Postpaid phone ARPU** — YoY change in dollars and %; is pricing power holding amid promotional intensity?
- **Postpaid ARPU (total, blended)** — includes phones + non-phone connections; lower than phone ARPU
- **Wireless service revenue** — strip out equipment to isolate the recurring stream; report YoY growth %
- **Equipment revenue** — note if upgrade cycle is boosting or depressing topline optics
- **Mix vs. promotion**: is ARPU growth coming from premium tier mix shift, or being eroded by aggressive promos? Pull management's framing.

## W3. EBITDA Service Margin

The industry-standard wireless profitability measure.

- **EBITDA service margin** = wireless EBITDA ÷ wireless service revenue (not total revenue, which is distorted by equipment)
- Healthy quarter: >40% for AT&T/Verizon, with T-Mobile typically highest among Big 3 post-Sprint synergies
- YoY change and trend direction
- Note any one-time items (restructuring, network costs, accounting changes) that affect comparability

## W4. Fixed Wireless Access (FWA)

Critical for Verizon and T-Mobile (less so for AT&T, which is fiber-led on broadband).

- **FWA net adds** for the quarter and **cumulative subscriber base**
- Progress vs. **management's FWA target** (e.g., T-Mobile and Verizon both have multi-year subscriber targets)
- **FWA ARPU** vs. fiber ARPU if disclosed (helps assess monetization quality)
- Competitive positioning: is FWA cannibalizing cable, attracting new-to-broadband, or pulling from fiber competitors?
- Capacity considerations: management commentary on whether FWA growth is supply-constrained by 5G capacity in dense markets

## W5. Fiber & Broadband

Varies meaningfully across the Big 3 — see company reference for specifics.

- **Fiber net adds** — absolute and YoY trend
- **Fiber subs / passings / penetration** — penetration = subs ÷ passings; rising penetration = maturing market
- **Total broadband net adds** — fiber + FWA + legacy copper/DSL (net of copper losses)
- **Broadband ARPU** — fiber ARPU vs. legacy; mix shift to fiber is a tailwind
- **Fiber + mobile bundle attach** ("converged customers") — central to AT&T and Verizon's strategy; rising attach = lower churn, higher ARPU

For T-Mobile specifically: fiber is via JVs (Lumos, Metronet acquisition); report progress but don't overweight — wireless + FWA is the core story.

## W6. 5G Network & Spectrum

- **5G coverage** by band (low-band, C-band / mid-band, mmWave) — population covered
- **Spectrum holdings update** — any new licenses, auctions (e.g., AWS-3, C-band, 600 MHz), buildout milestones
- **Network rankings** — Opensignal, Ookla, RootMetrics if cited; T-Mobile has led 5G rankings; Verizon historically led 4G
- **Standalone 5G (SA)** progress — enables network slicing, enterprise use cases
- **Enterprise 5G / private network** wins if disclosed

## W7. Convergence Strategy

The central strategic battleground. Every Big 3 carrier has a stated convergence narrative.

- **Bundle attach rate** — % of mobile customers with broadband (or vice versa)
- **Churn benefit** — converged customer churn vs. single-product churn (often disclosed in slides)
- **Convergence-driven ARPU lift** — converged customers typically have meaningfully higher household ARPU
- Management framing: is convergence a primary thesis, or just a tactical bundle?

## W8. Capex & Capital Intensity

- **Total capex** for the period; **capex as % of revenue** (capex intensity)
- Direction: rising (investment cycle), peak (mid-buildout), or declining (harvest mode)?
- **Wireless capex vs. wireline/fiber capex** if disclosed
- Management capex guidance for the year and trajectory commentary
- Big 3 capex cycles: AT&T post-fiber-peak; Verizon post-C-band-peak; T-Mobile post-Sprint-integration

## W9. Free Cash Flow

- **FCF after capex** = Operating cash flow − capex
- **FCF margin** = FCF ÷ revenue
- Compare to **management's full-year FCF guidance**
- **Working capital and one-time items** — spectrum payments, litigation, severance — can distort FCF; flag them
- YoY FCF trend

## W10. Balance Sheet & Capital Return

Big 3 carriers carry meaningful debt; balance sheet is a thesis driver, especially for AT&T and Verizon.

- **Net debt** — absolute figure and trend
- **Net debt / EBITDA leverage ratio** — compare to stated target (AT&T ~2.5x, Verizon ~2.25x)
- **Interest expense** — large and rate-sensitive; note refinancing activity
- **Dividend** — DPS, total cash cost, **FCF dividend coverage** (FCF ÷ total dividends paid); >1x cushion is needed
- **Buybacks** — Big 3 historically prioritized debt paydown over buybacks; flag if any meaningful buybacks resumed
- Asset sales / spectrum monetization / DirecTV stake updates (AT&T-specific)
