# T-Mobile (TMUS) — Company-Specific Module

Idiosyncratic content for T-Mobile. Universal telecom KPIs are covered in `modules/wireless-led.md` — do **not** repeat those here. This module covers only what's distinctive to T-Mobile's segments, named programs, corporate actions, and stated strategic narrative.

---

## Segment Structure (Reporting Quirks)

T-Mobile reports as **a single consolidated business** — no formal operating segments. KPI splits are by line of business in supplemental materials:

- **Postpaid services** (the core)
- **Prepaid services** (Metro by T-Mobile, plus Mint and other DTC brands)
- **Wholesale & other services** (DISH MVNO arrangement, etc.)
- **Equipment revenue**
- **High Speed Internet (FWA)** — broken out in supplementals
- **T-Mobile Fiber** — small, broken out separately

T-Mobile is effectively a **wireless pure-play** for segment-reporting purposes. Don't expect AT&T- or Verizon-style segment tables.

## Idiosyncratic Programs & Assets

### Sprint Integration (Legacy)
The Sprint merger closed April 2020. By 2024, **integration synergies were largely realized** ($7.5B+ targeted in run-rate cost synergies). The Sprint asset base — particularly **2.5 GHz mid-band spectrum** — is the foundation of T-Mobile's 5G mid-band leadership. Track:
- Any residual integration costs or one-time items
- Cell-site decommissioning progress (legacy Sprint sites)
- Magenta MAX migration of legacy Sprint subs

### Magenta MAX Premium Tier
T-Mobile's premium unlimited plan. **Magenta MAX adoption / mix shift** is a structural ARPU lever:
- % of postpaid base on Magenta MAX (when disclosed)
- ARPU lift from premium-tier mix shift

### Mint Mobile + Ka'ena Acquisitions
T-Mobile acquired Ka'ena Corporation (parent of Mint Mobile, Ultra Mobile, Plum) in May 2024 for ~$1.35B. Mint is a **DTC prepaid brand** (Ryan Reynolds–backed). Track:
- Prepaid net add contribution from Mint
- DTC channel economics

### Fiber JVs (Lumos, Metronet)
T-Mobile entered fiber via:
- **Lumos JV** (announced 2024 with EQT) — Southeastern fiber buildout
- **Metronet acquisition** (announced Apr 2024, ~$4.9B) — Midwest fiber operator with ~2M passings

These are **small relative to T-Mobile's wireless base**. Treat fiber as **convergence optionality**, not a near-term financial driver. Track passings + subs growth, but don't anchor the snapshot here.

### DISH MVNO Arrangement
DISH Network (Boost / EchoStar) has an MVNO arrangement on T-Mobile's network as part of the Sprint deal terms. **Wholesale revenue from DISH** appears in T-Mobile's wholesale line. Track for any disclosure on the relationship's evolution.

### 5G Network Leadership
T-Mobile has consistently led **Ookla / Opensignal 5G** rankings (speed, availability) post-Sprint integration. Network-quality leadership is a thesis pillar. Watch for:
- Continued ranking leadership (or any erosion)
- Standalone 5G deployment progress
- 2.5 GHz coverage percentage

### Capital Return Inflection
T-Mobile **shifted from deleveraging mode to aggressive buybacks** in 2023–2024. Capital return cadence is now a major thesis driver. Track:
- **Total $ buyback** in the period
- YoY share count reduction
- Authorization size remaining
- Dividend (initiated 2023; track any growth)

## Stated Strategic Thesis (Three Pillars)

Use these as the three "Where They Stand" bullets:

1. **Postpaid share gainer** — Un-carrier brand + 5G leadership + price advantage taking consistent net add share; track whether the lead is widening, holding, or compressing
2. **FWA + convergence buildout** — High Speed Internet scaling to multi-year target; fiber JVs adding optionality
3. **Capital return cadence** — Aggressive buybacks + initiated dividend; shareholder yield is now central to the equity story

## Recent Context (verify against current documents)

- Sprint integration synergies largely realized by end-2024
- T-Mobile **initiated dividend** in late 2023 — relatively new; payout is small compared to AT&T/Verizon
- Mint Mobile / Ka'ena acquisition closed May 2024
- Metronet acquisition announced April 2024, completion expected late 2024 — verify status
- T-Mobile has structurally **lower capex intensity** than AT&T or Verizon (no fiber buildout, no mass C-band-equivalent ongoing deployment)
