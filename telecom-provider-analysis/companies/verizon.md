## Segment Structure (Reporting Quirks)

Verizon reports two operating segments — different from AT&T's segment-by-product approach:

- **Verizon Consumer Group** — All consumer services: wireless (postpaid + prepaid), Fios fiber broadband, FWA for residential, video/legacy. The largest segment by revenue and EBITDA.
- **Verizon Business Group** — All business services: wireless and wireline to enterprise, public sector, SMB, wholesale, global accounts. Includes the wholesale revenue from cable MVNOs (Xfinity Mobile, Spectrum Mobile run on Verizon's network).

Note: Wireless and wireline are **not separated at the segment level** — they're inside Consumer or Business. KPI breakouts (postpaid phone net adds, fiber net adds, FWA net adds) are typically in supplemental materials, not in the primary segment table.

When extracting wireless metrics, watch for the split between:
- Consumer wireless retail postpaid (largest)
- Business wireless retail postpaid
- Wholesale (cable MVNO contribution) — separate line, structurally important

## Idiosyncratic Programs & Assets

### TracFone (Largest US Prepaid)
Acquired November 2021 for $6.9B. Verizon is the **largest US prepaid wireless operator**. Track:
- Prepaid net adds and churn — meaningfully different dynamic than postpaid
- Prepaid ARPU pressure (typically lower-end)
- Cross-sell from prepaid to postpaid (a Verizon thesis lever)

### Frontier Acquisition (Pending — Verify Status)
Announced September 2024 for ~$20B (~$38.50/share). Adds approximately **2.2M fiber subs** and ~7.2M fiber passings. As of the time this skill was authored, the deal was **pending regulatory approval and shareholder vote** — verify current status from the documents. If pending, track:
- Regulatory progress and expected close timing
- Synergy commentary from management
- Pro forma fiber footprint expectations

If closed, track integration progress and contribution to consolidated fiber adds.

### C-Band Buildout
Verizon was the largest spender in the C-band auction (2021, ~$45B + clearing costs). The C-band buildout has been the centerpiece of Verizon's 5G capex cycle. Track:
- **C-band population coverage** and progress vs. plan
- **FWA capacity unlock** in C-band markets (FWA growth has been C-band-buildout-dependent)
- Capex trajectory as C-band peak passes

### Public Sector / Federal
Verizon has structural strength in federal and public-sector wireless contracts (less centrally branded than AT&T's FirstNet, but meaningful). Watch for:
- Notable federal wins or losses
- Public-sector contribution to Business segment

### Cable MVNO Wholesale
Comcast (Xfinity Mobile) and Charter (Spectrum Mobile) run on Verizon's network. **Wholesale revenue from cable MVNOs** offsets some of the direct retail wireless pressure cable mobile creates. This is a unique structural feature — Verizon both competes with and supplies cable mobile.

## Stated Strategic Thesis (Three Pillars)

Use these as the three "Where They Stand" bullets:

1. **Premium network defense** — leveraging spectrum (especially C-band) and network quality to defend ARPU and reduce churn; willing to lose some volume share to TMUS rather than chase price
2. **FWA + fiber broadband scale** — first-mover FWA scaling to multi-million-sub target; Frontier deal transforms fiber footprint
3. **Capital return discipline** — maintain longest US-telecom dividend-increase streak; deleverage toward ~2.25x target while sustaining dividend
