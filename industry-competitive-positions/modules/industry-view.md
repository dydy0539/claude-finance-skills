# Industry View Module

Use this module when the user asks for an industry-wide view, the competitive landscape, "Big 3 vs cable", "where's the industry headed", or when 4+ providers' earnings are present together.

The output is a synthesis across the five providers — not a sum of individual snapshots. The goal is to surface **structural dynamics** of the US connectivity industry: who's gaining, who's losing, what the capex cycle looks like, and what the central strategic battlegrounds are.

---

## Industry View Output Structure

### Section 1 — Industry Structure Snapshot

A compact frame for the reader:

- **Wireless market** — three facilities-based postpaid carriers (AT&T, Verizon, T-Mobile) plus cable MVNOs (Xfinity Mobile, Spectrum Mobile) increasingly taking postpaid net add share. Approximate postpaid phone subscriber share if known.
- **Broadband market** — cable incumbents (Comcast, Charter) historically dominant in their footprints, now structurally pressured by **fiber overbuild** (AT&T Fiber, Frontier, Verizon Fios + Frontier acquisition, others) and **FWA** (Verizon, T-Mobile). Approximate broadband subscriber share if known.
- **Convergence** — every provider is pushing converged mobile + broadband bundles, but with inverted strategies: Big 3 anchor on wireless and add fiber; cable anchors on broadband and adds mobile.

State market shares qualitatively if exact figures aren't in the documents.

### Section 2 — Wireless Dynamics

- **Aggregate postpaid net adds across the industry** — total Big 3 + cable MVNO adds for the period. Use this to frame whether wireless growth is accelerating, stable, or decelerating overall.
- **Share of postpaid net adds**: rank the five providers by postpaid (or mobile line) net adds. Cable's share of the postpaid pie is a structural shift indicator — when cable takes 30%+ of net adds, the Big 3 are losing share to lower-priced bundled offerings.
- **ARPU dynamics across the Big 3** — are all three holding pricing? Diverging? Note any signs of promotional escalation.
- **Churn trends** — rising for who? Falling for who? Rising churn at Big 3 + accelerating cable mobile adds is the clearest "share migration" signal.
- **Wireless capex** — has the 5G buildout cycle peaked? Are the Big 3 converging on similar capex intensity?

### Section 3 — Broadband Dynamics

This is the most contested layer of the industry currently.

- **Aggregate residential broadband net adds across all providers** — sum cable losses, fiber gains, FWA gains. Is the total addressable broadband market growing, flat, or shrinking?
- **Cable broadband performance** — how negative are net adds? Is the rate of loss stabilizing or worsening?
- **Fiber overbuild progress** — AT&T Fiber, Verizon Fios + Frontier, others — net adds and share of new connects. Penetration trajectory in overbuilt markets.
- **FWA progress** — Verizon and T-Mobile FWA net adds together; aggregate FWA subscriber base; share of broadband net adds.
- **Substitution math** — qualitatively (or quantitatively if data allows) tie cable losses to fiber + FWA gains. Where is cable losing customers going?
- **Pricing dynamics** — broadband ARPU stability across providers; any signs of pricing pressure?

### Section 4 — Convergence

The strategic battleground.

- **Big 3 convergence**: which carriers are showing the strongest fiber + wireless bundle attach gains? Convergence-driven churn benefits if disclosed.
- **Cable convergence**: mobile attach to broadband across Comcast and Charter; is the bundle slowing broadband churn?
- **Cross-channel implication**: is convergence reducing churn industry-wide (good for everyone) or just shifting customers between providers (zero-sum)?
- **Pricing of converged bundles**: any escalation or rationalization of bundle pricing?

### Section 5 — Capital & Cash Flow Cycle

- **Where the industry sits in the capex cycle**: 5G buildout (mostly past peak); fiber buildout (mid-cycle for Big 3); cable network upgrades (mid-cycle, DOCSIS 4.0 ramping). Aggregate capex direction across providers.
- **FCF inflection**: are Big 3 carriers converging on FCF expansion as capex declines? Cable FCF profile — still high, but are buybacks stable or compressing?
- **Leverage trajectories**: which providers are deleveraging, which are stable, which are leveraging up. Industry interest expense exposure.
- **Capital return**: aggregate dividend + buyback across the five — is the industry returning more or less cash to shareholders YoY?

### Section 6 — Strategic & Structural Themes

Identify the 3–5 themes most clearly visible in the period's results. Examples (use what's actually in the data):

- **Cable's wireless share gain pace** — is the Big 3 → cable mobile migration accelerating, stable, or showing signs of saturation?
- **FWA's ceiling** — is FWA growth showing supply-side constraints (capacity) yet?
- **Fiber overbuild economics** — are fiber penetration curves looking healthier than expected?
- **Promotional intensity** — escalating or rationalizing?
- **Convergence as moat** — is converged-customer churn meaningfully lower, and is that translating to industry-level churn declines?
- **Regulatory / policy backdrop** — spectrum auctions, BEAD broadband subsidies, M&A approvals (e.g., Verizon-Frontier) — flag if material.
- **AI / enterprise data demand** — any commentary on enterprise/data-center connectivity demand flowing through these providers?

### Section 7 — Industry Bottom Line

3–5 sentences:
- The single most important shift in the period
- Where the industry's competitive momentum is concentrated (which providers, which segments)
- The biggest structural risk or watch-item for next period

## Notes for the Industry View

- **Aggregation requires care**: don't sum ARPU, sum subs and revenue. State the aggregation method when summing across providers.
- **Some figures will be unavailable** — not every provider discloses the same metrics. Use what's there; note gaps.
- **Use your knowledge to fill in industry context** when the documents lack it (e.g., approximate postpaid shares, broadband shares) — but flag when you're using your own knowledge vs. document data.
- **Avoid double-counting**: cable mobile MVNO subs run on Verizon's network, but they're attributed to Comcast/Charter for share-of-customer purposes. The wholesale revenue Verizon receives is reported in Verizon's results, but the customer relationship is the cable company's.
- **Period alignment matters even more for industry view** — providers may report different quarters; explicitly state which period each provider's data represents and any timing distortions.
