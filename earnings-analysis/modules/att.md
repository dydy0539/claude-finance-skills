# AT&T (T) — Company-Specific Module

Read this module when analyzing AT&T earnings. It extends and partially overrides the telecom module with AT&T-specific context, segment structure, and investment thesis hooks.

---

## AT&T Context

AT&T operates two primary reportable segments:
- **Mobility** — Wireless services (postpaid, prepaid) and equipment sales; the largest and most profitable segment
- **Consumer Wireline** — AT&T Fiber broadband, legacy copper DSL/voice; strategic growth engine
- **Business Wireline** — Enterprise and government connectivity; structurally declining but cash-generative

Key thesis backdrop: Post-WarnerMedia/DirecTV divestitures, AT&T repositioned as a pure-play connectivity company focused on wireless + fiber. The investment narrative is built on: (1) fiber subscriber ramp, (2) wireless service revenue growth, (3) deleveraging, and (4) FCF recovery.

---

## A1. Mobility Segment

- **Postpaid phone net adds** — the single most watched metric; compare to Verizon and T-Mobile for context
- **Postpaid phone churn** — AT&T has historically been weakest here among the Big 3; any improvement is thesis-positive
- **Postpaid ARPU** — is pricing holding amid competitive promotions?
- **Mobility service revenue** — YoY growth rate; consensus usually ~3–4% in normalized environment
- **Mobility EBITDA** and **EBITDA service margin** — should be >40% in a healthy quarter
- **FirstNet** — government/public safety network; subscriber count and any milestones disclosed

---

## A2. Consumer Wireline / AT&T Fiber

- **AT&T Fiber net adds** — the most-watched wireline metric; consensus-sensitive
- **Fiber passings added** in the quarter and cumulative total vs. ~30M target
- **Fiber penetration rate** = fiber subs ÷ fiber passings; watch for penetration curve inflection
- **Broadband ARPU** — fiber ARPU vs. legacy broadband; mix shift is a revenue tailwind
- **AT&T Fiber + Mobility bundle attach rate** — "converged" customers have lower churn and higher ARPU; rising attach is structurally bullish
- **Total broadband net adds** — net of fiber gains and legacy copper losses
- **Consumer Wireline EBITDA margin** — still recovering; trajectory matters more than level

---

## A3. Business Wireline

- Revenue trend — expect continued YoY decline (~mid-single digits); the question is whether the pace is stabilizing
- EBITDA contribution — still meaningful cash generator despite revenue decline
- Any notable enterprise wins, 5G private network deals, or FirstNet business contracts
- Management commentary on the pace of secular decline

---

## A4. Leverage & Balance Sheet (Critical for AT&T)

AT&T carries significant net debt — this is a key overhang and monitoring point:

- **Net debt** — report absolute figure and trend
- **Net debt / EBITDA leverage ratio** — AT&T's stated target is ~2.5x; report actual vs. target
- **Interest expense** — large and rate-sensitive; note any refinancing activity
- Progress toward leverage target: is AT&T on track or behind?
- Any asset sales, spectrum transactions, or DirecTV stake monetization updates

---

## A5. Free Cash Flow & Dividend Sustainability

FCF is the core valuation anchor for AT&T:

- **FCF after capex** — compare to management's full-year FCF guidance
- **Dividend** — current annual dividend per share and total cash cost
- **FCF dividend coverage ratio** = FCF ÷ total dividends paid; should be comfortably >1x
- Has management reaffirmed FCF guidance? Any changes to capital return policy?
- Capex trajectory: AT&T guided to lower capex post-fiber buildout peak; confirm progress

---

## A6. Competitive Context

AT&T operates in a three-player wireless market and a two-player fiber market (vs. incumbent cable):

- How do AT&T's postpaid net adds compare to Verizon and T-Mobile this quarter? (use your knowledge if not in the documents)
- Is cable (Comcast Xfinity Mobile, Charter Spectrum Mobile) taking wireless share?
- Is AT&T Fiber gaining against Comcast/Charter in broadband, or losing ground?
- Any notable promotional escalation or pricing rationalization in the market?

## A7. AT&T Guidance Specifics

Use this as the pre-loaded "additional guided items" checklist when filling out core skill section 3e for AT&T. **AT&T is a full-year guider** — they do not issue quarterly revenue or EPS guidance, so the next-quarter table in core 3e will be largely empty. Focus the guidance analysis on the **full-year** guide table.

**AT&T's standard FY guidance metrics** (typically given on the Q4 call for the upcoming year, then refined each quarter):

| Metric | How AT&T typically frames it | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Wireless / Mobility service revenue growth | YoY growth % range (e.g., "low single digits") | The headline top-line metric for the wireless thesis |
| Adjusted EBITDA growth (consolidated) | YoY growth % range | Blended across Mobility, Consumer Wireline, Business Wireline |
| Adjusted EPS | Absolute range | Comparable to consensus; the most-tracked guidance metric |
| Capital investment | Absolute range, in $B | AT&T uses "capital investment" rather than "capex" — it INCLUDES capex AND vendor financing payments. Do not conflate with cash capex on the cash flow statement |
| Free cash flow | Absolute range, in $B | Defined excluding DirecTV cash distributions; the dividend-coverage anchor |
| Net debt / Adjusted EBITDA leverage trajectory | Multi-year target (~2.5x) | Sometimes framed as a milestone year ("reach 2.5x by [date]") rather than a period-end snapshot |

**Less consistently disclosed but watch for:**

- Mobility segment EBITDA growth (sub-segment guide if given)
- Consumer Wireline EBITDA inflection commentary (when does the segment hit sustained positive growth?)
- Business Wireline EBITDA decline pace
- Effective tax rate
- Pension / OPEB cash items if unusually large

**Forward IOMA notes specific to AT&T:**

- AT&T is a **mature, low-growth top line** — absolute revenue dollars don't move much YoY. This means **even small Adj EBITDA changes produce large (or negative) IOMA values**. The metric is still useful but interpret with that magnitude caveat: a 100%+ forward IOMA on a modest revenue change is normal for AT&T and does not carry the same meaning as a 100% IOMA at a high-growth software name.
- For Forward IOMA, prefer **Adj EBITDA** (which AT&T guides) over Adj OI (not directly guided in the FY framework). Note this choice in the output.
- **FCF growth IOMA** — `(Guided FCF − Prior FCF) ÷ (Guided Revenue − Prior Revenue)` — is worth computing as an AT&T-specific overlay since FCF is the valuation anchor. Not a standard core-skill metric but useful here.

**Beat-and-raise pattern at AT&T:**

AT&T has historically guided conservatively and tends to **maintain** (not raise) full-year FCF and Adj EPS guidance even after quarterly beats. A maintained guide after a beat is the norm, not a yellow flag. **A raised FY guide** at AT&T is unusual and thesis-positive — particularly mid-year raises on FCF or Adj EPS, which should be highlighted prominently in the qualitative read.

## A8. AT&T-Specific Bottom Line

Assess the quarter against AT&T's three-part thesis:
1. **Fiber ramp** — is the subscriber engine still firing? Passings on track?
2. **Wireless defense** — holding postpaid share and ARPU against cable MVNOs and T-Mobile?
3. **Deleveraging** — net debt declining toward 2.5x target? FCF guidance intact?
