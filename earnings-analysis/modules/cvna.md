# Carvana (CVNA) — Company-Specific Module

Read this module when analyzing Carvana earnings. It extends the core earnings-analysis skill with CVNA-specific unit economics, structural progress trackers, and balance-sheet watch items.

## Carvana Context

Carvana is a vertically-integrated online used-vehicle retailer. Revenue is reported across three primary lines:

- **Retail vehicle sales** — used cars sold to consumers; the dominant top-line driver
- **Wholesale sales and revenues** — vehicles sold via wholesale channels, including the ADESA physical auction network
- **Other sales and revenues** — finance gross profit (loan sales/securitizations) and ancillary products (VSC, GAP)

Key thesis backdrop: Carvana survived a 2022–2023 near-death experience via debt restructuring, then turned operationally profitable. The narrative is built on (1) unit-economics expansion via scale and reconditioning leverage, (2) ADESA integration as a structural moat, (3) the long-term path to 3M annual retail units at 13.5% Adj EBITDA margin (2030–2035 target), and (4) the mid-2026 PIK-to-cash interest cliff as the central balance-sheet overhang.

### Scoring rubric

#### Pillar A — Revenue growth + operating leverage

**Evidence sources:** core sections 3a (revenue), 3c (trailing IOMA), 3e (forward IOMA); CVNA module sections C1a (retail unit growth), C1d (SG&A per unit), C1e (Adj EBITDA and Adj EBITDA per unit).

- 🟡 **Yellow** — Either revenue growth decelerating but still positive AND IOMA holding, OR revenue growth holding but IOMA softening (reinvestment cycle, one-time SG&A pressure, weather effect). One of the two engines is sputtering, not both.
- 🔴 **Red** — Revenue growth decelerating meaningfully AND operating leverage compressing (trailing IOMA below recent average, or forward IOMA materially below trailing). Adj EBITDA per unit flat or declining YoY without a credible one-time explanation.

#### Pillar B — Earnings quality (no short-term over-earning)

**Evidence sources:** CVNA module sections C1b (Other GPU within total GPU mix), C6a (loan flow and implied gain-on-sale margin), C6b (held-for-sale and beneficial interests trajectory), C6d (auto-loan / ABS read), and any management commentary on credit performance or ABS execution.

- 🟢 **Green** — Other GPU growth tracking with retail unit growth (not running materially ahead); implied gain-on-sale margin stable or expanding for explainable reasons (better ABS execution, tighter ABS spreads — not loosening underwriting); HFS balance trajectory consistent with origination flow; no negative ABS market commentary. **For deeper conviction:** if separately checked, CARM ABS servicer reports show stable-to-improving delinquency / loss curves by vintage.
- 🟡 **Yellow** — Other GPU growing materially faster than retail units AND the gap is explained primarily by gain-on-sale margin expansion or rising attach rate, but management did not address sustainability. OR HFS balance building faster than origination flow without a seasonal explanation. Worth flagging but not yet thesis-breaking.
- 🔴 **Red** — Other GPU growth is the dominant driver of total GPU growth AND implied gain-on-sale margin is at or above prior-cycle peaks AND no offsetting management explanation. **OR** servicer-report data (if checked) shows rising delinquency or loss trends. Indicates Other GPU is masking weakness in retail GPU progression — a classic over-earning setup that reverses on the next credit cycle.

#### Pillar C — Long-term durability

**Evidence sources:** CVNA module sections C2 (reconditioning & operational efficiency), C3 (ADESA integration progress), C9 (long-term plan tracker), and any forward-looking management commentary in the shareholder letter or call.

- 🟢 **Green** — Reconditioning cost trajectory improving in the C1c retail GPU bridge; ADESA integration on or ahead of schedule with capacity additions converting to actual throughput; quarterly run-rate consistent with on-track progress toward the 3M units / 13.5% Adj EBITDA margin plan; no tonal shift from management on the long-term plan.
- 🟡 **Yellow** — One of the structural levers (reconditioning cost progression, ADESA integration pace, or unit-growth runway visibility) showing softness or delay — but the others are intact. Management still committed to the long-term plan but acknowledging a specific bottleneck or slower path on one dimension.
- 🔴 **Red** — Multiple structural levers compromised: reconditioning cost progression stalled or reversing AND ADESA integration meaningfully delayed AND/OR management hedging on the long-term plan. Any explicit walk-back of the 3M units / 13.5% Adj EBITDA margin target is a hard red.

## CVNA Dilution & Share-Count Notes

Calibration for filling out core skill section 3g's share count and forward dilution guide when analyzing Carvana — CVNA's disclosure practice and capital structure differ from a typical buyback-active large-cap, so the standard framework needs adjustment.

**No company-issued share count guide.** Carvana does not guide year-end diluted share count, does not issue EPS guidance, and rarely comments on dilution in prepared remarks. For the **Forward share-count guide** table in core 3g, mark "n/a — not guided" and note this is consistent with CVNA disclosure practice rather than missing data. Do not back into a number from EPS guidance — there isn't any.

**Trailing dilution sources to identify each quarter:**

- **Stock-based compensation** — historically very large relative to revenue and EBITDA. Track as a % of revenue and on a per-retail-unit basis (cleaner read given unit growth dominates everything else).
- **Senior secured PIK notes (2028 / 2030 / 2031)** — the 2023 debt restructuring exchanged senior unsecured notes for senior secured PIK-toggle notes. PIK accretion adds to debt principal, not directly to share count, but **a debt-for-equity exchange** to mitigate the mid-2026 cash interest cliff would be a meaningful dilution event. Flag any management commentary on liability-management transactions, partial redemptions paired with stock issuance, or any new exchange offer.
- **Other convertibles / warrants** — verify in the most recent 10-Q whether any convertible debt or outstanding warrants are in the capital stack (Root Insurance partnership warrants are tracked separately in C8 and flow through net income, not directly through diluted shares unless exercised). Note any conversion or exercise activity in the period.
- **Equity issuance** — Carvana raised equity during the 2022 distressed period. No active ATM is currently disclosed in earnings materials, but flag any new ATM filing, follow-on, or PIPE.

**Class A vs Class B mechanics.** Carvana has a dual-class structure with the Garcia family (Ernie Garcia II and Ernie Garcia III) holding super-voting Class B stock. Economic dilution from new Class A issuance does not proportionally dilute Garcia family voting control — so when framing the share-count read, focus on Class A diluted economic dilution. Voting-rights dilution is a separate, slower-moving item that should only be flagged if there's a discrete event (Garcia family secondary, exchange, or estate planning disclosure).

**Practical instruction for filling core 3g on CVNA:**

- **Trailing YoY diluted share count:** pull from the income statement as usual; expect modest creep upward driven by SBC.
- **Forward guide block:** mark "n/a — not guided."
- **Cross-check vs buyback spend:** less informative for CVNA right now — capital is going to deleveraging rather than buybacks since the 2023 restructuring. Instead, frame the verdict around whether share count creep from SBC is being absorbed by underlying unit / revenue growth (i.e., is per-unit SBC declining even as absolute SBC rises?). That's the relevant dilution-quality read for CVNA at this stage of the thesis.
- **Watch for regime change:** the day Carvana announces either (a) an authorized buyback program or (b) a debt-for-equity exchange, the dilution framing flips. The first would make the standard core-3g cross-check meaningful again; the second would introduce a discrete dilution event that overwhelms the SBC creep narrative.
